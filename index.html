Words first recorded in New Scientist

The Oxford English Dictionary gives New Scientist as its earliest citation for these 203 words and phrases. They are listed by year, each with the passage from the magazine that the dictionary cites.

An earliest citation is not proof of coinage. It means the dictionary has found no earlier use, and earlier examples may exist. Some of these passages show a term already in use, or report one coined by someone else. Many are technical terms or trade names.

Spot check of 23 terms

In November 2026, 23 of the better-known terms were checked against scanned periodicals in the Internet Archive, and against scholarly records for connectomics. Seven had an earlier use: desalination, flat screen, hover-car, scramjet, nail bomb, spintronics and connectomics. Those entries are marked below, along with two that could not be settled, computer crime and whistle-blowing.

Nothing earlier turned up for number-cruncher, acronymania, user friendliness, Pythonesque, games console, dongle, satellite TV, data protection, mood ring, motherese, green chemistry, terabit, VCR or brain chip. That is weak evidence: the search was brief, these archives are incomplete, and many hits were scanning errors. VCR could only be searched in its full form, and the other 180 terms were not checked.
